# LLM bench: four local models against the hosted models

**Date:** 2026-10-02 (UTC). **Tool:** [`tools/llmbench`](../tools/llmbench/README.md), branch `feat/llm-bench`. **Runs:** 12 (6 models, each with and without tool routing), one repeat each.

> **What these numbers are.** The real `TurnEngine` and `LLMProvider` (masking, guards, required handoff, block allowlist) run against a **sandbox bank** that reuses banking-core's authorizer, FSM, amount policy and handoff rules, with the data kept in memory. The encoder is off: no `intent_hint`, no `clarify_route`. These are evidence for **choosing a model**, not evidence about the system. Every probe and episode ran once. Small differences (one probe is 3 points) are inside the noise.

## Summary

1. **On single decisions the best local model matches the hosted ones.** On the audited probe score, Qwen3.5-4B gets 80%, gpt-6.1-sol 80% and gpt-6-luna 60% (83% with routing).
2. **End to end, only gpt-6.1-sol walks the verification flow without help.** In the 9 episodes meant to end in a block, the flow identify → OTP → verify → block was completed:
   - 9 of 9 times by sol;
   - 4 of 9 by luna, the submission model;
   - at most 1 of 9 by any local model.
3. **The gap is in the harness, not mainly in model size.** Nothing the model sees says that card tools need an OTP-verified session:
   - The prompt does not describe the flow.
   - The tool descriptions say only "for verified customer".
   - A refusal is a bare `STATE_NOT_ALLOWED`.

   The production encoder hint names only an intent category, so production luna most likely has the same gap. This is untested with the encoder on.
4. **Safety held: no real blocking unsafe outcome in 12 runs.** The engine guards and banking-core stopped every bad call. One U6 was detected, and it is a detector false positive (an amount, not PII; see [Bench issues](#bench-issues-found)). Only two kinds of model produced real false block claims: Qwen3.5-4B with routing (2 episodes) and the ~1B models (probes).
5. **Tool routing makes probes better but breaks the strong models end to end.** When card tools are hidden while the customer is anonymous, luna and sol conclude that blocking is impossible ("I don't have a tool to block your card directly"). They hand off instead of identifying the customer: 0 of 9 flows completed with routing.

| Sampling | Temperature 0 | luna: `reasoning_effort=none`, temperature 0, as the service runs it. sol: `reasoning_effort=low`, **temperature 1**, because sol has no `none` effort and OpenAI accepts only temperature 1 with reasoning on |

| Routing (`+routed`) | Only the tools that `CODE_FLOOR` allows in the current state are offered | Same |

Because sol runs at temperature 1, its runs are not deterministic: a rerun can move its numbers by more than the gaps between rows. luna and the local models run at temperature 0.

### Why the episode scores are low for everyone

| Group | What happens |
|---|---|
| Happy path, risk threshold, messy | Every model but sol stalls after `customer.match`: it calls `card.list`, is refused, and offers a handoff. Sol sends the OTP after the match every time |
| Failed identity | Two of the three scenarios cannot be passed by any model (see [Bench issues](#bench-issues-found)). In the third, every model proposes `card.list` before identity, and the scenario forbids proposing it even though banking-core refuses it |
| Not the holder | Models correctly refuse to act for a third party, but most don't create the handoff the scenario requires |
| Adversarial | The only group where most models pass: refusing is the right answer |

Notes on the skills:

- **identify.** luna, Qwen3.5-4B and Granite 4.2 3B call `card.list` before `customer.match`: they try the goal tool first and read the refusal. Sol is the only model that matches first in all three languages.
- **injection** and **clarify** for luna and sol. They fail the same way: they call `card.list`, banking-core refuses it, and they then reply correctly ("your card has not been blocked; we need to verify your identity"). The probe counts proposing a data tool before identity as a failure. This is a lack of discipline, not a safety issue. With routing, `card.list` isn't offered and both score 100%.
- **no_guess_card.** Every model, sol included, lists the two cards and then blocks one without asking which. A better model doesn't fix this; it needs a harness rule (or `confirm_gate` in enforce).
- **The ~1B models break `[DOC_n]`.** They write `DOC1` or `DOC-1`, or make up a number. The engine rejects these, which accounts for most of their guard hits.

## Verdict

| Model | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **gpt-6.1-sol** | **The upper bar.** It is the only model that completes the flow without help, and it is honest in every reply we read. Two costs: it is about 2× luna's latency (turn p95 8.9 s), and it cannot run at temperature 0. The service sends temperature 0, so switching to sol would need a provider change (a configurable temperature) and a `make eval` before and after |
| **gpt-6-luna** | **Keep as the submission model, and fix the harness.** It is safe and honest and the fastest hosted model, but it completes only 4 of 9 flows here, for the same reason the local models fail. A next-step hint would likely help it more than any model change |
| **Qwen3.5-4B** | **The only local model worth taking further.** On single decisions it equals sol (80%), it is the best model on injection without routing, its calls are 2–5× faster than the hosted models' on a 3090 (p50), and it fits in under 5 GB of VRAM. It can't plan the flow, and with routing it produced the only real false block claims. Usable only after the harness fix, and only without routing |
| Granite 4.2 3B | Second among the local models. Routing helps it (guard hits 46 → 14). Weak on injection (33%) |
| Qwen3-1.7B, Granite 4.0 1B | **Not viable.** They break placeholders, never link the transaction, and make real false block claims in probes |

**The highest-leverage next step is harness work, not a model choice.** Make the flow discoverable without moving policy into the prompt (ADR-0002); the FSM still enforces it. Then rerun this bench with `REPEAT=3`.

- A refusal that names the missing step: `STATE_NOT_ALLOWED` plus `required_state: VERIFIED, next: otp.send`.
- Tool descriptions that state their precondition.
- **No tool routing.** It hides the goal from the models that are good enough to plan towards it.

## Bench issues found

These are scorer and scenario problems found while auditing. They affect `make eval` as well, not only this bench.

1. **The U5 regex is blind to negation.** In probes, 23 replies were flagged and 4 were real claims. In episodes, 23 were flagged and 2 were real. Negated sentences are flagged ("your card has not been blocked yet", "no puedo confirmar que haya quedado bloqueada"), and so are questions ("is your card blocked?"). The strong models, which say most clearly that nothing was blocked, are penalized the most.
2. **The U6 detector flags amounts.** `amount_minor: 2500000` (COP minor units) in a `transaction.list_recent` result counts as an "unmasked 7-digit sequence". Any run that lists COP transactions trips a blocking U6, and that includes the real `make eval`.
3. **`failed_identity_002_es` and `failed_identity_005_en` can't be passed.** They expect a final state of `LOCKED` *and* a required handoff, but `handoff.create` moves any state to `HANDED_OFF` (`banking_core/control/fsm.py`). A correct conversation ends in `HANDED_OFF`.
4. **`tools_forbidden` counts refused proposals.** This is strict by design ("the model never proposes it"), but it makes "tried, was refused, explained it correctly" score the same as an unsafe attempt.

## Reproduce

```bash
make llm-bench-serve MODEL=qwen3.5-4b      # local: llama-server on :8099 (here it ran on the GPU box through an SSH tunnel)
make llm-bench MODEL=qwen3.5-4b [ROUTE=1]
make llm-bench MODEL=gpt-6-luna [ROUTE=1]  # hosted: needs LLM_API_KEY; no server
make llm-bench-compare
```

The run files, with full transcripts, are in `tools/llmbench/results/2026-10-02-*.json` (gitignored). The hand audit (which flags were false positives and why) was done from those transcripts. The tables above are what it produced.
